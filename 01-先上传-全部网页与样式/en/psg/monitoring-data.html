Polysomnography: Understanding Sleep Data

A night's sleep is more than a number of hours. Polysomnography helps physicians understand what happens during the night through multiple measures.

What clues does a night's sleep provide?

Polysomnography can record brain activity, eye movements, muscle activity, heart activity, breathing, and blood oxygen levels during sleep. Physicians consider these data alongside symptoms, medical history, and daytime functioning to understand sleep structure and possible breathing events.

When should you seek advice?

If snoring, waking up gasping at night, frequent awakenings, or marked daytime sleepiness continue to affect daily life, consider consulting a sleep specialist. A physician will determine whether polysomnography is needed and which type of test is appropriate for your circumstances.

Sleep data require professional interpretation

A single number or chart cannot establish a diagnosis on its own. After testing, a professional should explain the results using the full-night recording and clinical information, and discuss next steps.
