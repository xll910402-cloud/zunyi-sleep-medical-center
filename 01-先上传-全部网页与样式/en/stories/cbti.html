Better Sleep Is Within Reach: Understanding CBT-I

Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) addresses sleep habits and thoughts about sleep to help people gradually establish a more stable sleep pattern.

What does CBT-I involve?

CBT-I typically includes stimulus control, sleep restriction, cognitive restructuring, sleep hygiene, and relaxation training. Stimulus control aims to rebuild the association between bed and sleep. Cognitive restructuring addresses thoughts that can heighten anxiety before bedtime. Sleep hygiene covers relatively regular sleep schedules and a suitable sleep environment.

Relaxation and behavioral exercises

Breathing exercises, muscle relaxation, and body scans may form part of a professionally guided plan. Sleep restriction involves adjusting time in bed according to actual sleep patterns and should not be attempted independently using a fixed duration found online. Causes of insomnia and accompanying conditions differ from person to person, so the approach also needs to be adapted.

When to seek help

If insomnia persists and affects daytime study, work, or daily life, or is accompanied by significant anxiety or low mood, you can seek an assessment at a sleep clinic. Healthcare professionals will consider symptoms, daily habits, and any necessary tests when discussing an appropriate individual plan.

A note about seeking care

Health education articles and patient stories provide an overview of care approaches. Specific tests and treatments should be based on a professional assessment.
