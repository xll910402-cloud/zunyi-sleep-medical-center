Long-Term Insomnia and Safely Reducing or Stopping Sleep Medication

When insomnia persists despite repeated changes to sleep medication, the priority is to reassess the problem and develop a safe plan that can be followed up.

Reassessing insomnia

Long-term insomnia and medication management often involve several factors. Alongside difficulty falling asleep, there may be sleep-related breathing problems, emotional distress, or irregular routines. A physician will review medical history, current medications, any necessary sleep monitoring, and psychological assessment to identify factors that may be affecting sleep.

Adjust medication gradually

If an existing plan is not working well or medication-related problems arise, a physician may gradually adjust medicines under medical supervision, alongside behavioral guidance, sleep hygiene education, and other suitable interventions. Medicines, doses, and physical conditions vary, so the pace of dose reduction or discontinuation also varies. Another person's experience should not be copied.

Do not stop medication or increase the dose on your own

Increasing a dose on your own, stopping medication abruptly, or taking several sedative medicines at once may carry risks. If you are concerned about tolerance or dependence, or experience daytime discomfort, speak with the prescribing physician or a sleep specialist and follow the agreed plan for follow-up and adjustments.

A note about seeking care

Health education articles and patient stories provide an overview of care approaches. Specific tests and treatments should be based on a professional assessment.
