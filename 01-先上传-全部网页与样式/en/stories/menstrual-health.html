Menstrual Pain Deserves More Than Endurance

Menstrual pain that gradually worsens, disrupts study or daily life, or comes with other menstrual changes deserves a hospital assessment to identify the cause.

Primary and secondary dysmenorrhea

Primary dysmenorrhea is usually associated with physiological changes during menstruation. Secondary dysmenorrhea may be related to conditions such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, or pelvic inflammatory disease. Pain intensity alone cannot tell you which is the cause.

When to seek a timely assessment

If pain keeps worsening, affects study or daily activities, or is accompanied by changes such as substantially heavier or longer periods, seek an assessment from a gynecologist or another appropriate specialist. Repeatedly increasing pain medication on your own may mask a problem that needs attention.

A multidisciplinary approach

The sleep care team and gynecology department may work together on multidisciplinary assessment and appropriate care for related concerns. Improvement in one case does not establish a general treatment effect; a physician will determine the specific plan based on test results.

A note about seeking care

Health education articles and patient stories provide an overview of care approaches. Specific tests and treatments should be based on a professional assessment.
