World Sleep Day Multidisciplinary Free Clinic

Under the theme “Quality Sleep, Better Life,” the center joined several specialty teams to offer sleep health consultations and on-site screening to the public.

Different specialties addressing sleep together

The sleep care team joined colleagues from otolaryngology, neurology, respiratory medicine, and a team working on brain-computer interfaces at the free clinic. They answered questions about common concerns such as snoring, difficulty falling asleep, light sleep, and daytime fatigue, and explained sleep monitoring and how to access follow-up care.

Bringing sleep education beyond the clinic

The event offered health education materials and talks about sleep. It also introduced services such as sleep monitoring, psychological assessment, physical therapy, and traditional Chinese medicine nursing care. On-site consultations helped people understand that sleep problems may require assessment by several specialties. Healthcare professionals should determine specific tests and treatments according to individual circumstances.

Continuing the service

A free clinic can help identify sleep concerns that warrant further attention. Repeatedly waking up gasping, insomnia that substantially affects daytime functioning, or sleep problems accompanied by emotional distress still require a full hospital assessment.

A note about seeking care

Health education articles and patient stories provide an overview of care approaches. Specific tests and treatments should be based on a professional assessment.
